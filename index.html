ALSEP

Apollo Lunar Surface Experiments Package: science stations left on the Moon by Apollo astronauts.

What was left behind

The stations kept sending data until operations ended in 1977.

Impact on researchers today

Researchers still reanalyse Apollo seismic and surface data, and NASA has worked to recover and archive data that was once lost.

ALSEP: full case file

  1. Mission recordsApollo 12, 14, 15, 16 and 17 each left a station of science instruments on the Moon. Apollo 11 left a simpler package.
  2. EvidenceYears of seismic and surface measurements sent to Earth until operations ended in 1977. Some of the data was archived and some was lost.
  3. AnalysisNASA teams worked to recover and restore lost ALSEP data so it can be used again.
  4. FindingsOld lunar data keeps answering new questions about the inside of the Moon.

More sources

  • Restored Apollo data (Planetary Data System)
  • NASA report on recovering lost ALSEP data

Verdict: the mission ended, but the evidence remains.

Surveyor 3: full case file

  1. Mission recordsSurveyor 3 landed on the Moon in April 1967 and sent back images and soil measurements.
  2. EvidenceIn November 1969 Apollo 12 astronauts removed the camera, the scoop and other parts and returned them to Earth.
  3. AnalysisTeams studied how the materials changed after about two and a half years on the lunar surface.
  4. FindingsReturned hardware shows how materials age in space, which informs how future spacecraft are built.

More sources

  • NASA analysis of the returned Surveyor 3 parts (PDF)

Verdict: the mission ended, but the evidence remains.

Ingenuity: full case file

  1. Mission recordsIngenuity landed on Mars in February 2021 with the Perseverance rover and flew 72 times.
  2. EvidenceFlight logs, images and engineering data from every flight, up to the final one in January 2024.
  3. AnalysisEngineers study what worked and what failed to plan aircraft for other worlds.
  4. FindingsPowered flight on Mars is proven, and its data guides future aerial explorers.

Kepler: full case file

  1. Mission recordsKepler launched in 2009 and stared at a single patch of sky to find planets around other stars. It retired in 2018.
  2. EvidenceBrightness measurements of more than 150,000 stars.
  3. AnalysisResearchers keep searching the archive for planets that earlier searches missed.
  4. FindingsKepler showed that planets are common, and its archive is still producing discoveries.

Verdict: the mission ended, but the evidence remains.

Opportunity: full case file

  1. Mission recordsOpportunity landed on Mars in January 2004 and kept driving until a dust storm ended the mission in 2019.
  2. EvidenceImages and measurements of rocks and soil along its long route.
  3. AnalysisScientists used them to understand where liquid water once existed on Mars.
  4. FindingsIts findings help decide where future missions look for signs of ancient habitable places.
